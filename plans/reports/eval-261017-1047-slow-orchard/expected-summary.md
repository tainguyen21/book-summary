# Expected summary and rubric — "The Slow Orchard"

Written before the run, so the rubric cannot be tuned to the output.

## Reference summary

Between 2019 and 2024, volunteers in Merrow Bay (about 6,200 people) called
the Orchard Circle planted 140 free-to-pick fruit trees on unused public land.
The book's core claim is that planting is easy and keeping trees alive is the
real work. A May 2019 frost killed 17 of the first 38 nursery trees because
they were raised in a milder climate; switching to grafting cuttings from
local survivors cut losses to under 9 percent (9 of 102), and frost cloth
was shown not to help. Of four sites, the school slope and cemetery edge
worked, and the harbour roundabout failed (4 of 14 survived) and was given
back. Three rules emerged: every tree needs a named waterer for two summers,
pruning happens only on one fixed Cutting Day in late February, and fruit is
never sold, with unpicked fruit going to the food bank and school kitchen.
Harvests grew from about 0.8 to 1.9 to 3.1 tonnes (2022–2024), though only
the donated share was weighed. The orchard cost about 9,400 euros over five
years, mostly covered by sapling sales; it is not free to the town (the
council pays about 600 euros a year for insurance) but costs less than the
roughly 1,500 euros a year of mowing it replaced.
